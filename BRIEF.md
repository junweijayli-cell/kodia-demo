# KORDIA: the outdoor room

Self-authored under explicit creative delegation. The user asked for professional web and graphic design judgment, a more three-dimensional scroll experience inspired by Fora, and an independently deployed demo. The original repository and domain must remain unchanged.

## Evidence and authored decisions

1. Vibe: authored as architectural, sunlit, tactile, spacious. User reference: https://fora.so/.
2. Journey: authored as enter an outdoor room, pause on the value of outdoor living, discover the Signature family, inspect real materials, explore the catalogue and settings, then start an enquiry.
3. Energy: authored as a composed opening, quiet text, one dimensional furniture reveal, compact practical content, assured close.
4. Feeling: curiosity in the opening scene; calm in the short introduction; delight when a furnished terrace grows from an architectural frame; confidence in material closeups; agency in the catalogue; readiness at the enquiry.
5. Signature move: the terrace aperture. Architectural side planes open away as the scene moves toward the visitor and furniture occludes a large background word. It resolves into a labelled Signature collection.
6. Aesthetic: retain KORDIA's official logo and self-hosted Urbanist/Stardom families. Deep navy, mineral white, and restrained brand red. Warmth comes from existing furniture imagery, rather than cream and brass UI.
7. Structure: distinct scenes, not one continuous flight. Existing hash-routed product, settings, factory, materials, shipping, contact and enquiry screens remain usable.
8. Assets: the original repository's Signature renders, catalogue, project photos, material images, logo and fonts. Original README notes that some catalogue image rights were unconfirmed; preserve that documentation. Signature renders remain identified as visualisations. No fabricated product specs or new performance claims.

## Feeling curve, written before the scroll score

Curiosity: an outdoor composition with spatially separated typography, architecture and furniture.
Calm: a short, generously spaced statement about making room for life outside.
Delight (peak): a terrace opens toward the visitor; foreground furniture passes in front of the oversized word, then settles into an object label.
Confidence: close material photography with direct links to existing care and finish information.
Agency: real collections and settings lead into existing filtering, product details and saved selections.
Readiness: a resolved invitation to discuss the selected pieces, connected to the existing enquiry wizard.

Peak sentence: “The terrace opened up and the furniture seemed to come out of the page.”
Tell-someone sentence: “It's the site where you step through a frame into an outdoor room.”
Silence: the short introductory statement is intentionally calm. No empty viewport or loading pause is authored.

## Grammar and fingerprint

Custom grammar: architectural showroom. Navigation is a persistent room-directory bar with direct catalogue access. A short spatial entrance leads to a quiet wall of type; the largest span belongs to a walk-through Signature display. Material and catalogue browsing is natural flow. The ending is a solid enquiry wall with the same action as the navigation. Bans: autoplay slideshows, continuous flights, repeated pinned argument sections, decorative counters, custom cursors, and empty scroll spans.

The existing eight grammars were considered: filmic one-shot prevents catalogue jumps; chaptered editorial keeps all media below the title; live surface fits software; continuous world requires an unbroken location; typographic poster suppresses furniture; gallery/catalog disallows the marketing introduction; split stage imposes an irrelevant comparison; episodic cuts prioritize hard interruption. The showroom structure preserves the existing commercial site while adding the requested spatial reveal.

The local fingerprint registry is empty. This first build has no prior rows to collide with.
